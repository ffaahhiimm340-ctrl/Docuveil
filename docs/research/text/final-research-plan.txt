Final Research Plan — Personal Document Integrity & Consistency
Oct 12, 2026 · @Omkar
Summary
We build a Flutter app (Windows + Android) that keeps a person's important documents organized and safe, and we research one engine behind it: owner-side, field-level checking of personal documents. The same engine answers two questions nobody currently answers for an ordinary person: has this document been changed, and exactly how? and do my documents agree with each other before I apply for something?
Final contribution list
Field-level tamper detection (main). Automatically reports which named field of a document changed, with old and new values, and separates harmless changes (re-save, re-scan, compression) from real tampering, across the owner's own devices.
Owner-side cross-document consistency audit (new). Before an application, checks that name, date of birth and other fields agree across the person's Aadhaar, PAN, marksheets and passport, using Indian naming rules (initials, name order, post-marriage surnames), and tells them which document to fix.
Private cross-device sync. Keyed fingerprints so the relay server cannot read documents or tell which document an alert is about.
Availability-aware alerts. A per-document score (copies, backup, integrity) sets alert urgency.
Benchmark dataset. Synthetic Indian personal documents with labelled tampered versions, harmless versions and cross-document mismatch cases.
Contributions 1 and 2 carry the paper. 3 to 5 support them. Malware scanning, ransomware guard, expiry reminders and search are app features we build but do not claim.

Contribution B: Owner-side cross-document consistency audit
The re-check found that comparing fields across a person's identity documents has been published, so this contribution must be worded carefully. What remains open is doing it for the owner, before they apply, with Indian naming rules and fix-it advice, instead of producing a pass/fail trust score for a verifier.
The problem is real and common. Name, gender or date-of-birth mismatches make PAN–Aadhaar linking fail, and the link is required to file income tax returns (Paisabazaar). Causes include a father's initial on PAN but the full name on Aadhaar, apostrophes in surnames, and full stops between initials (Business Insider India). NTA advised JEE Main 2025 applicants to make their Aadhaar name match their application (Vedantu), and initials placed differently on certificates and Aadhaar can complicate passport applications (Careers360).

What we add
Runs on the owner's device over all their documents at once, before any application.
Classifies each difference: equivalent (initial expanded, order swapped, case/punctuation), likely problem (spelling variant, maiden vs married surname), blocking (different DOB or gender).
Explains the fix: which document to update, in which order, for which purpose (PAN–Aadhaar link, passport, exam).
Caution on [28]: it is a low-tier journal and some of its references look wrong (it credits the OCR-graph-features paper to authors other than James, Gupta and Raviv). Cite it as related work, and do not rely on its numbers.

Limitations to state in the paper
Only 5 document types and 2 platforms; Android sandboxing limits what can be monitored.
Synthetic data only; real-world OCR noise may be worse.
Name rules cover common Indian patterns, not every regional convention or script.
The relay server still sees timing and message counts.
Decoy-based ransomware detection can be bypassed by decoy-aware ransomware [23].
Do not claim: content hashing, perceptual hashing, template comparison, two-file comparison, cross-document identity matching in general, HMAC identifiers, decoy files, criticality-based alerts, or "the first Indian document dataset". Say "to our knowledge" rather than "first".

Confirmed novelty statement
Use this in the Introduction after round 2:
Existing document-integrity methods rely on the issuer: a signature with change rules, a printed integrity code, or registration with a government locker. Others judge a single image without an original, or compare document versions on behalf of a verifier, through manual comparison tools, lender fraud engines, or document-image change-detection research. Identity-consistency checks exist, but they run for banks and verifiers, not for the person who holds the documents. To our knowledge, no system lets an individual, without issuer cooperation, automatically learn which named field of their own document changed and whether the change was harmless, coordinate such alerts across their devices without revealing documents to a server, and audit their own documents for cross-document mismatches before applying for a service.
Contributions, in order of strength
Owner-side field-level tamper detection with a harmless/tamper verdict (no issuer signature or code required).
Owner-side cross-document consistency audit with Indian naming rules, grounded in official rules such as UIDAI's [40], and fix-it advice.
Keyed-fingerprint cross-device alert protocol (vs plain-hash approaches such as [36]).
Availability-aware alert urgency for one person (vs admin-configured tiers [37]).
Synthetic benchmark with tamper pairs, harmless pairs and cross-document mismatch sets.
Do not claim (add to the earlier list): rule-based detection of disallowed changes to signed PDFs [35], cross-device hash comparison for corruption or ransomware [36], tiered notifications by asset priority [37], PII consistency scoring [39], or change detection between document versions in general [42, 43].
Related Work (draft for the paper)
Bracketed numbers match the reference list below. Rewrite in your own voice before submission.
Personal information management. Semantic desktop systems integrated personal files by meaning on a single machine [1, 2], and reviews show people still struggle to manage documents spread across devices [3, 4]. Consumer vault apps now add encrypted storage, OCR and expiry reminders, but none of these verifies whether a document's content has changed.
Issuer-based document integrity. Integrity codes computed over a document's substantive content can be printed on it and re-verified from an OCR'd copy [5], and per-region integrity maps extend this to parts of a page [6]. Adobe's signed-PDF mechanism lets an author define which fields may change and flags disallowed differences between the signed and current states [35]. India's DigiLocker verifies documents pushed by registered issuers, but not documents users upload themselves [26]. All of these depend on the issuer acting at creation time.
Reference-based and forensic tamper detection. Template methods store features of an original and flag changed regions in a scanned copy [7]; fuzzy and perceptual hashes tolerate print–scan noise [8]. Forensic models localize tampered text from a single image without any original [9, 10], and synthetic ID datasets support training them [11, 12]. Commercial tools compare two files that a user selects [27], lender-facing fraud engines show where and how a submitted document was altered [43], and change detection between document-image versions is an established research area [42]. None report which named field changed, separate harmless changes from tampering, or run automatically for an individual.
Cross-device integrity and private identifiers. A consumer backup patent compares file hashes across many users' devices to flag a common file changed on one device and detects ransomware by modification rate [36]. Plain hashes, however, allow confirmation-of-file attacks; convergence secrets, server-aided keys and HMAC identifiers mitigate this in storage systems [13–15]. These have not been applied to tamper alerts for one person's unique documents.
Alert prioritization. Enterprise tools weight alerts by administrator-assigned asset criticality [24], backup systems issue tiered notifications by asset priority [37], and file value to a user can set backup order [38]. Recovery systems prioritize restores by criticality [25]. None compute a per-document availability score to set an individual's tamper-alert urgency.
Cross-document identity consistency. KYC platforms and patents check whether name, date of birth and address agree across documents and sources [30, 31, 39], India-focused APIs match names despite initials, order and transliteration [32, 33, 41], and academic work scores the correlation of a person's identity documents [28, 29]. All of these serve the verifier after submission. Name mismatches remain a common cause of failed PAN–Aadhaar linking, where UIDAI requires exact date of birth and gender [40].
Malicious documents and ransomware. Static and ML detectors identify malicious PDF and Office files [16–18]; ransomware defences include behavioural monitoring [19, 20] and decoy files [21, 22], which decoy-aware ransomware can evade [23]. Our system integrates these as a baseline layer only.
Gap. To our knowledge, no prior work gives an individual owner-side, field-level tamper explanations with a harmless/tamper verdict, private cross-device coordination, and a pre-application cross-document consistency audit, without relying on any issuer.
